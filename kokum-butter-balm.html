Why a balm, not a lotion

Made to stay where it's needed.

Most lotions are mostly water. They feel nice, then evaporate. A balm is all butters and oils, so it stays on the skin that needs it. Slide to see what happens when it meets your hand.

The ritual

Two minutes, every night.

  1. Warm

    Scoop a small amount and warm it between your fingertips until it softens.

  2. Massage

    Work it into clean, dry heels, elbows and knees with slow, firm strokes.

  3. Rest

    Let it sink in overnight. With steady use over a few weeks, skin feels softer and smoother.

Full ingredient list

Six ingredients. Every one explained.

In label order, heaviest first, with what each one is and why it's in the jar.

Read: What is kokum butter?

  1. Rice Bran OilOryza Sativa (Rice) Bran Oil

    What it is: A light oil pressed from the outer layer of the rice grain.

    Why it's here: The base of the balm. It sinks in easily and carries the butter across the skin.

  2. Caprylic/Capric TriglycerideDerived from coconut

    What it is: A light, silky oil made from coconut.

    Why it's here: Helps the balm glide on without a heavy, greasy feel.

  3. Kokum ButterGarcinia Indica Seed (Kokum) Butter

    What it is: A firm butter from the seeds of the kokum fruit, which many Indian families know first from the kitchen.

    Why it's here: Solid at room temperature, it softens on contact with warm skin. It softens and conditions dry, rough skin.

    Good to know: It isn't cold-pressed. Our supplier uses a process suited to extracting the solid fat from kokum seeds.

  4. BeeswaxCera Alba

    What it is: Natural wax made by honey bees.

    Why it's here: Keeps the balm solid in the jar and in place on skin. It's also why the balm isn't vegan.

  5. Vitamin ETocopherol / Mixed Tocopherols

    What it is: An antioxidant found naturally in plant oils.

    Why it's here: A small amount helps keep the oils fresh.

  6. Cinnamon Leaf OilCinnamomum Zeylanicum Leaf Oil

    What it is: An essential oil from the leaf of the cinnamon tree.

    Why it's here: Gives the balm its warm scent, at a low level.

    Good to know: We switched from bark oil to leaf oil after looking at how each behaves on skin.

No. It contains beeswax, which keeps it solid in the jar and in place on skin.
