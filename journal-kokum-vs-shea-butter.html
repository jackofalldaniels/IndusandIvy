Kokum butter vs. shea butter for dry feet

Shea is the butter most people know. Kokum is the one we grew up with. Here is how they actually differ, and why we chose kokum for our balm.

By the Indus & Ivy founders  ·  October 2026  ·  5 min read

Walk down any body care aisle in the US and you'll see shea butter everywhere. Kokum butter is much harder to find. People sometimes ask us whether kokum is "better." The honest answer is that they're different, and the difference shows up in how they feel.

Where they come from

Shea butter comes from the nuts of the shea tree, Vitellaria paradoxa, which grows across the savanna belt of Africa. Most shea in stores is West African; there's also a softer East African variety, often called shea nilotica.

Kokum butter comes from the seeds of the kokum fruit, Garcinia indica, a tree native to the Western Ghats in India. You can read more in What is kokum butter?

Side by side

Kokum butterShea butter (West African)
Main fatty acidsStearic 55–65%, oleic 30–44%Oleic 40–55%, stearic 35–48%
At room temperatureVery firm, can crumbleSofter and creamier
Starts to meltAbout 34–40°CAbout 35–42°C
Skin feelOften described as light and less greasyRich and cushiony

The short version: kokum has more stearic acid, so it's firmer. Shea has more oleic acid, so it's softer. East African shea has even more oleic acid and melts at a much lower temperature, which is why it feels almost like a whipped cream.

What that means for dry feet

Both butters are emollients. Both soften and condition dry skin. We haven't seen good research showing that one does a better job on heels than the other, so we won't claim that it does.

Where you'll notice a difference is in texture and feel:

  • If you like a rich, cushiony layer, shea tends to feel more substantial on the skin.
  • If you want something that softens on contact but doesn't leave a heavy film, kokum is worth trying. It's a popular choice in balms for exactly this reason.
  • If you're making a solid balm that has to stay put, kokum's firmness helps it hold its shape in the jar and on the skin.

Different butters, different feel. Neither one is magic.

Why we chose kokum

We'll be upfront: part of the reason is that kokum is ours. It's an ingredient we grew up around, and very few brands in the US use it as the hero.

The other part is the texture. We wanted a balm that's firm in the jar, softens as you warm it between your fingers, and doesn't feel slick under socks. Kokum does that well, especially paired with a light oil like rice bran oil.

If you already love shea, you don't need to switch. If you're curious what all of this feels like in practice, that's what we made the balm for.
